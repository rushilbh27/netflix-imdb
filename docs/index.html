Scores in. Fluff out.

Netflix hides IMDb ratings, so NoFluff puts them back on every title. It also hits Skip for you on YouTube ads and on Netflix and Prime Video intros, recaps and credits, even in fullscreen.

No tracking. No server. Skip

Netflix hides the one number you actually want.

You scroll, you guess from the artwork, you give a show twenty minutes. IMDb already knows whether it is any good. Netflix just does not show you.

Extensions that fixed this existed, and then Netflix changed its page and they stopped working.

Why this one keeps working

Most Netflix extensions find titles by CSS class names. Netflix regenerates those every few releases, so the extension silently dies.

This one looks for links like /watch/ and Netflix's own test hooks instead, and every Netflix-specific selector lives in one object. When Netflix does change something, the fix is a few lines.

Two minutes to set up, once.

There is no server, so ratings need your own free key. That is the whole trade, and it is why nothing gets tracked. Skipping needs no key and works the moment it is loaded.

    Load the extension

    Download it from GitHub and load it unpacked in Chrome. A setup page opens by itself.

  2. 2

    The setup page walks you through it: one form, one email, one click. Then paste the key into the ticket.

    A badge appears on every title card. Hover for votes, Rotten Tomatoes and Metacritic. Click to open IMDb.

Skip the fluff.

NoFluff presses Skip the instant YouTube, Netflix or Prime Video offers it. No countdown stare, no hunting for the button, and it works in fullscreen.

Ad · Sponsored Watch this 30-second ad for a thing you do not need. Ad skipped

Press Skip. The extension does exactly this, on its own, on the real sites.

  • YouTube

    Skippable video ads, static image ads, and the closable ad banners.

  • Netflix

    Skip Intro and Skip Recap.

  • Prime Video

    Skip Intro, Skip Recap and Skip Credits.

It never skips what you cannot skip.

NoFluff is not an ad blocker. It only presses the Skip button the site itself offers, and it will not click "Next episode".

One honest quirk.

YouTube ignores clicks made by scripts, and static ads have no video to jump past. For those, NoFluff sends a real click through Chrome's debugger API, so Chrome shows a "started debugging this browser" bar for a few seconds. That is the only reason it asks for the debugger permission.

Yours to switch off.

The toolbar popup has a switch per site, a switch per skip type, and a counter of what it skipped today.

Small on purpose.

Plain JavaScript, no dependencies, no build step. You can read all of it in an afternoon.

Scores on every card, details on hover

A badge at the bottom-left of each title: green at 7.5 and up, yellow to 6.0, red below. Hover for the IMDb score with vote count, Rotten Tomatoes and Metacritic. Open a title and all three sit above the details.

Lookups a day, free

Your own OMDb key, cached for 30 days, fetched only for cards on screen. Normal browsing barely scratches it.

No server. No tracking.

Your browser talks to OMDb directly, and skipping never touches the network. No analytics, no remote fonts, no third-party scripts. Skipping reads nothing from the pages; it only presses Skip.

Built for the next redesign

Cards are found by routing links and test attributes, not CSS classes. Loud console warning if detection ever breaks.

Free and open source

MIT licensed. Read it, fork it, fix it. If Netflix changes its page and something breaks, a pull request is usually a handful of lines.

Setup that holds your hand

The setup page shows exactly what to fill in on OMDb, mocks the email so you know which part to copy, and catches the usual mistake of copying the activation code. Paste the whole email and it finds the key.

Install it.

It is not in the Chrome Web Store, so you load it yourself. Takes about two minutes.

  1. Get the code. Clone the repo, or download the ZIP and unzip it somewhere it will stay.
  2. Open chrome://extensions and turn on Developer mode (top right).
  3. Load unpacked and pick the nofluff folder (nofluff-main if you downloaded the ZIP).
  4. Follow the setup page that opens. It takes you through getting your free key, which is only for ratings.
  5. Open Netflix, YouTube or Prime Video. Badges and skipping start on their own. Refresh any tab that was already open.

Questions.

Is it actually free?

Yes. The extension is MIT licensed. The ratings come from OMDb, whose free key allows 1,000 lookups a day. There is no account with us and nothing to pay.

Why do I need my own key?

Because there is no server in the middle. Your lookups use your quota and go straight from your browser to OMDb. That is also why nothing about what you watch can be collected: there is nowhere for it to go.

What does it see and send?

For ratings, it reads titles on the Netflix pages you browse so it can look them up, and sends those title strings to OMDb with your key. Your key and cached ratings stay in your browser. For skipping, it only looks for the Skip button on YouTube, Netflix and Prime Video and presses it; nothing from those pages is read out or sent anywhere. No analytics, no telemetry. It is all unminified, so you can check.

Why is it not in the Chrome Web Store?

It is not published there, so you load it unpacked from GitHub. Chrome may nag about developer-mode extensions now and then. That is normal for any unpacked extension.

Which browsers work?

Chrome, Edge, Brave, Arc and Opera. Not Firefox or Safari.

What if a rating is wrong?

Netflix does not expose IMDb IDs, so titles are matched by name, and that is never perfect. When the match looks doubtful you get a ? instead of a number. If you spot a wrong one, open an issue with the title and what the hover card said.

What happens when Netflix changes its page?

Eventually something will break. The extension warns in the console when it finds no title cards, and every Netflix-specific selector is in one place, so a fix is small. Issues and pull requests are welcome.

Does it skip every YouTube ad?

Every ad YouTube lets you skip, yes: video ads and static image ads, plus the closable banners. It does not block ads, and an ad with no Skip button stays put. It presses Skip the moment the button is there.

What is the "started debugging this browser" bar?

YouTube ignores clicks made by scripts, and static ads have no video to jump to the end of. For those, NoFluff sends a real click through Chrome's debugger API, which is the only reason it asks for the debugger permission. Chrome shows its own bar for a few seconds while that happens. It only ever targets youtube.com, and the bar goes away by itself.

Does it work on Prime Video or Disney+?

Skipping works on Prime Video: intro, recap and credits. Ratings are Netflix only for now, and Disney+ is not supported yet. The structure would allow more, and it is on the list of good first contributions.
